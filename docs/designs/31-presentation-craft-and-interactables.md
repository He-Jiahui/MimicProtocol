# Presentation Craft and Interactables

Status: Design proposal. Builds on [Rendering and Visual Style](11-rendering-and-visual-style.md) and [Presentation, Creatures and City Terrain](29-presentation-creatures-and-city-terrain.md). [Back to index](README.md)

After a shot hits a delivery van, the operator should be able to tell what it struck. Bright flecks skip off the metal door; safety glass whitens around the impact; loose glass falls inward, leaving a dark opening. The sound dies away while rain keeps running down the panel. A growing luminous sphere would give all those materials the same response. A simple warning outline still has a job: it can tell the player where danger will land without pretending to be the impact itself.

**Effects that belong to Meridian.** Let a vent's plume curl under the awning and break in the wind at its edge. Let venom cling to a dry wall and streak on a wet one. Procedural effects fit the [city's existing art direction](10-procedural-generation.md); reusable, openly licensed effect elements are another possibility where their terms have been checked for the intended use. No particular pack is assumed. Give the effect detail where it meets the world, and leave enough quiet pavement around it that a web warning stays visible under the neon.

**Models that explain a gesture.** From above, an evacuee tucks a bag under their coat; from the shoulder, a hand actually holds the strap. Give the van wheel wells, inset glass and a sliding door with somewhere to slide. A street tree grows around an old pruning scar, with a lopsided crown toward the light; beneath it, ferns fill the damp gap behind a planter while the worn footpath stays clear. Character joints, bodywork and plant growth offer more variety than covering the same silhouette in different textures. Keep human disguises as carefully observed as civilians.

**Things the operator can use.** A van with a live cab might shelter evacuees for a [pickup](34-raid-pressure-and-field-challenges.md); a wreck with a crushed cab can still have a searchable rear compartment. A service door gives under the handle, a jammed shutter rattles against its track, and a dead lift has a rain-stained closure notice. Those differences tell the player what is worth trying. An open van door takes up real space in the alley; the other approach to the shelter should still be visible. These local interactions give vehicles a role without promising that every parked car can be driven.
