# Class Resource Loops

Status: Design research and proposal. Updated: 2026-11-26. Requirements: R-01, R-04, D-21, D-22.

[Back to index](README.md) | Previous: [Brushwild skill-tree research](27-skill-tree-reference-brushwild.md)

**Proposal:** Make a resource answer a question the player can see in the room. A Rifleman looking through a doorway might fire at the marked Mimic now, or roll sideways and wait for the pack to cross behind it. Both shots are worth taking. The skill tree should change which shot the player looks for.

## Why Charge seldom decides the fight

John reports that the bar usually looks full. The demo opens with a spender already available. Aimed hits, passive recovery while searching, a dodge that avoids a hit, and refunds for consuming a Brand all feed Charge. Meanwhile M2 has to come off cooldown before it can spend again. A successful R opens Overdrive: M2 is free when available and builder hits are more productive. Ordinary fighting can therefore refill the bank while the player is waiting on something else. This explains the report without assuming every class or build reaches full equally often. See `src/sim/raid.js`: `Raid.updatePlayer`, `damagePlayer`, `consume`, `castSpender`, `useSkills` and `startOverdrive`.

The shared economy is easier to see than the class payoff. In `src/ui/hud.js`, `Hud.update` shows Charge or the Overdrive timer in the same orb; `drawOverlay` puts the Brands on enemies. In `src/sim/data.js`, Feedback Loop rewards Consume and Overflow Valve even rewards staying full. Blade can earn a shorter wait for a move it hasn't used yet. Toxic Handler needs a living, heavily poisoned target still inside a cloud for Rupture. By contrast, when a marked target bursts among its neighbors, the player can see what their aim bought them.

The distinction matters for the “Gunner” idea. Rifleman uses **Suppression** for a piercing shot and possible ricochet. The literal **Mark** that sentries and drones turn into splash belongs to Summoner. Both are live in `src/sim/skills.js` and `Raid.updateSummons`; the proposed Rifleman pack burst below extends the first idea while preserving the second. [Demo kits](18-demo-classes-elites-and-items.md) and [Skill Resource and Synergies](24-skill-resource-and-synergies.md) describe the shared design. `SKILL_KITS` in `src/sim/skill-data.js`, `compileSkills` in `src/sim/skill-build.js`, and `castSkill` in `src/sim/skills.js` identify what actually runs. The catalog of planned variants is separate.

## What to inherit from StormBound

John's StormBound **RESULT_CARD**, in its *Class differences* and *Costs, cooldowns, charges and interruption* sections, distinguishes what travels with the operator, what stays on a living enemy, and what lies on the ground. Its Huntress can leave feathers along a route; its Drifter can carry recovered scrap away. Its Enforcer can turn a guarded impact into shield energy. Its REX makes an actual life trade, and its Acolyte distinguishes a blood reserve from a body on the floor. These are examples from the adopted browser designs. Where the resource lives changes the decision: I can keep my reserve when I retreat, but the opportunity on that enemy may leave through another door.

The card's *Stacking, refreshing, exclusivity and elemental reactions* distinguishes future damage from damage already dealt. That gives poison a useful question: let the infection work, refresh it, or take what remains as a burst? *Class differences* also describes acid pools that matter to movement and attacks. A **blood pool** below is a DarkSignal proposal combining that ground play with the life trade. The card's *Acquisition, learning, unlocking and configuration* describes equipped variants that change a native action or link slots. That is a basis for choosing between a quick release and a prepared burst inside the same kit.

**Source boundary:** As the card's *Version differences* explains, early rank documents are history; the original editor proposal and its teaching examples do not define the live classes. Archived evidence describes particular observations. The untracked draft called **MIMIC PROTOCOL** is neither a StormBound mainline contract nor evidence for this DarkSignal checkout. The card was read as documentation; no StormBound checkout or Caps were used.

## Proposal: what each operator looks for

**Rifleman — a marked target with company.** Keep aimed M1 bursts painting Suppression. M2 could snap an early mark into a tight burst among nearby Mimics: enough to clear the doorway without waiting for a perfect ricochet. If the street beyond is filling, S offers a better firing angle; holding ready rifle cells for R could turn the barrage into a committed sweep through the marked pack. The gunner chooses the center of the clear with the reticle. Today `burstRifle`, `piercingLine` and `barrage` already give that decision a vocabulary of bursts, lines and cones.

**Blade — an opening worth staying in.** Let ordinary M1 contact build momentum and show an enemy losing its guard. M2 could spend that opening on a quick whirl to make breathing room. Imagine a shield fragment knocked loose by a cut, still lying on the far side of a living brute: S can lunge through it for cover to keep fighting, or carry Blade straight out of danger. Staying close with momentum in reserve makes R a heavy execution worth committing to. This grows from `threeStrike`, `lunge` and `execution`; the everyday payoff would be the visible opening, even when there is no kill or cooldown to refund.

**Summoner — a firing position worth supplying.** Give field hardware and radio orders separate budgets. An aimed M1 grenade paints the operator's choice of target; a sentry placed with M2 can turn a quick radio order into a local Mark burst. Spending supplies on that sentry should leave the operator able to call shots. S might hop beside the emplacement to redirect its fire as the pack turns. Alternatively, keep supplies for the crossing: R could commit a prepared drone flight to the marks while the operator moves with it. Radio traffic can run dry and recover quickly; the decision about where to leave hardware should outlast it. `markerGrenade`, `tacticalHop` and `Raid.updateSummons` already make placement and marked targets relevant.

**Toxic Handler — an infection worth leaving alive.** M1 spit on an approaching Mimic should give M2's cloud something to rupture now, even in a small skirmish. At a crowded choke, S could lay a pool across the pursuit and give the infection time to spread; R could release that accumulated harm across the pack. An optional shortcut: risk some of the operator's own health to prime a blood pool when there's no time to cultivate one. With a full bag and extraction in sight, keeping the health and taking the small rupture should be a good call. `toxicSpit`, `acidSlide`, `witherCloudArea`, `plagueBurst` and `Raid.updateDots` supply the existing distinction between a poisoned enemy, dangerous ground and a lethal release.

## Proposal: enemies, ground and the walk to extraction

A plated Mimic bracing in a shop entrance could take **high frontal damage reduction**. An ordinary shot from the front still wounds it. Spending enough of a saved reserve to turn the attack into a heavy hit could punch through more reliably; a flank offers a clean angle without that commitment. At high damage, the operator should be able to mow through the facing brute and its escort. A plate ringing under fire is useful feedback; an immunity flash would teach the wrong lesson.

Let the room keep some of the combat history. A fragment across an exposed aisle can tempt Blade out of cover; a blood pool at the stairs can invite the Handler to fight there or leave it behind. Give combat drops a silhouette distinct from gear worth extracting. Keep boss openings useful without requiring disposable adds. These distinctions protect the search and loot decisions in [Loot and Contamination](08-loot-contamination-and-legendary-gear.md), [Demo Bosses](22-demo-bosses.md) and [Boss Loot](23-demo-boss-loot-and-drops.md).

Between rooms, carried supplies can travel, a mark can disappear with its host, and melee momentum can cool while the operator searches. Waiting in a cleared room should buy neither a prepared heavy hit nor an obligation to hurry past the intel. Keep S useful for escape and M1 manually aimed in both views, as in [Camera and Combat](04-camera-controls-and-combat.md) and [Classes and Skills](05-classes-and-skills.md). Retire the universal free-spend Overdrive window; let R pay off what this operator prepared. The HUD can show readiness where the player is already looking: a loaded cell on the rifle, an exposed guard on the brute, a fragment on the floor.

## Proposal: a skill tree for the kit you brought

Give DarkSignal a persistent class tree in the Barracks. From StormBound take variants of native actions and links between slots. From the [Brushwild tree study](27-skill-tree-reference-brushwild.md) take readable alternatives: show what choosing a branch gives up, let the player preview a swap, and make returning to a familiar build straightforward. [Meta Progression](07-meta-progression-and-difficulty.md) gives that choice a home. The rifle tuning in `SKILL_TUNING` is an existing example of a choice the compiler can express; `newProfile` in `src/sim/profile.js` and `showHub` in `src/ui/hub.js` do not yet offer a player-owned tree.

A Rifleman might trade splash centered on the marked enemy for discharge along the firing line; rolling for an angle becomes more valuable than aiming at the densest knot. Blade might choose between collecting a fragment on the way **in** for an execution or on the way **out** of a whirl for a guarded retreat. A Summoner might route a mark order through the emplacement instead of the moving drones, trading pursuit for a dependable firing lane. A Handler might keep an infection alive by refreshing it in a pool instead of taking its immediate rupture. Each choice changes where the player aims, moves or spends with the original kit.

Avoid a power ladder, mandatory health or damage purchases, and branches that turn the operator into a second class. Neither reference tree needs to be copied. A base kit should already deliver its signature in an ordinary fight. Needing a rare drop, a talent, a perfect dodge or a boss that spawns fodder just to see it is a sign the base loop needs work.

**Later order:** Start by easing back Charge from routine builder hits, leaving aimed attacks and escapes available. Does spending now make the next M2 a decision while fighting? Next, distinguish the class payoffs in ordinary rooms, then put them under pressure from a flank, a tempting pickup or a lone boss. Revisit Charge-dependent gear as those payoffs replace it. Only then offer tree variants for choices players already recognize. These are proposals for the browser WebGPU demo; their pacing and readability have not been playtested here.
