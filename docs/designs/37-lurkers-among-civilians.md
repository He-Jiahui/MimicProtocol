# Lurkers Among Civilians

Status: Design proposal. Revisits the damage reveal in [Mutants, Detection and Enemy AI](03-mutants-detection-and-enemy-ai.md) and [Camera, Controls and Combat](04-camera-controls-and-combat.md). [Back to index](README.md)

A courier hurries toward the operator outside a shuttered clinic. They could be asking for help. Across the street, a pack that was barely moving has started to press against the clinic doors. That is evidence worth investigating; it is not proof that the courier is responsible. A disguised lurker matters most when what it is doing to the street gives the player a reason to find it.

**What changes.** Today `Raid.damageEnemy` in `src/sim/raid.js` calls for a reveal when damage reaches a disguised Mimic. It also records civilian kills; `applyRaidResult` in `src/sim/profile.js` deducts a cash penalty. `Raid.doScan` already puts the rig on cooldown and opens links. John's concern is the weight of those choices in the street. A debrief deduction may mean little beside a bagful of loot; a wounded dispatcher dropping the radio and fleeing before she can call the rescue van changes the next decision immediately.

**A deliberate shot is a commitment.** For a damage-triggered reveal, an aimed hit has to finish the apparent human body. A lesser wound doesn't identify it: the figure can flee still disguised, just as an injured civilian might. Finishing a disguise exposes a living elite; it doesn't award a kill or drops for the discarded shape. Area harm leaves everyone who still looks human untouched. Splash, lingering pools, stray chains and automatic sentry fire can't be used to test a crowd. A grenade specialist could deliberately strike the chosen figure with the projectile itself; the burst around it cannot expose anyone else. Give each kit a deliberate aimed option, including a directed close strike. No auto-aim on basics.

**Scan is a different way to know.** The rig can identify and reveal a lurker without harming a civilian; keep its recovery long enough to make spending the scan here a choice. Keep the [mutual link](01-world-and-narrative.md): the creature knows it has been made. Hostility need not mean running straight at the operator. Every reveal brings out an elite. The kind of threat should fit the region and how late the raid has become, and defeating it should offer a better drop than an ordinary street enemy. Searching the crowd for loot alone should be a poor bargain compared with keeping the people and the route intact.

**Reasons to confront one, or let it go.**

- A hidden lurker under a bus shelter might steady nearby monsters or sap the operator's movement. The disturbance is perceptible without identifying its source. On reveal it may turn and fight, or try to reach another pack. Breaking the aura can be worth more than the elite drop.
- A scanned lurker might head for a queue of evacuees. Left alive, it can gather them close and assimilate them. A clear call from the clinic gives the operator a chance to draw the people away while deciding whether to pursue. Killing it early keeps the [escort](34-raid-pressure-and-field-challenges.md) possible.
- A close approach might end in a heavy ambush, a brief stunning grip, or an elite deliberately revealing to detonate. Let the dropped disguise and its committed gesture announce the attack. Ordinary humans also approach for shelter; proximity alone is no verdict. Under [Staged Damage Calculation](30-staged-damage-calculation.md), the blow and the bind have distinct consequences. Losing movement needn't secretly amplify the same impact, and one detonation needn't pay for another hit on contact.
- A lurker with a stolen dispatch radio could flee toward a service tunnel to misdirect the pickup. Recovering the radio or warning the real driver is a reason to break pursuit. A crowd that saw the operator shoot an innocent courier might refuse to pass the warning on. Losing that help changes the route home; it shouldn't erase every way to extract.
