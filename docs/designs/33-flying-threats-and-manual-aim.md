# Flying Threats and Manual Aim

Status: Design proposal. Extends [Camera, Controls and Combat](04-camera-controls-and-combat.md) and [Combat Feel](25-combat-feel-reference-brushwild.md). [Back to index](README.md)

A winged Mimic clings to the underside of a [skybridge](32-upper-floors-and-the-way-down.md). When it lets go, the shop sign behind it disappears and its shadow slides across the wet road. The operator can take a difficult shot on the crossing, shelter under the bridge or wait for the creature to flare its wings before it lands. A flier earns its place by changing that decision. A ground enemy drawn higher up would leave the player guessing why the shot missed.

**From above, let the player aim through the air.** The chest-height plane in the earlier camera design is a useful reference for street fights. A flier's shadow can locate it over the street, but aiming at the shadow should still put a round into the pavement. A small sight mark on the chosen line of fire could show where it meets the creature's height. If it banks away, the shot continues through the space it left. When a pedestrian passes underneath, holding the higher angle should keep the round overhead; lowering it is a deliberate change of aim. Allow the player to pick the empty gap between them. No basic attack snaps to a target.

**From the shoulder, let the gun follow the sight.** Leaning out at a stairhead, the operator can raise the muzzle toward the underside of the bridge or lower it toward a creature skimming the canal. A railing in front of the barrel can still catch the shot even if the camera sees over it. A chip in the railing explains the miss better than a hit marker on the flier. Switching views should preserve which opening the operator meant to shoot through.

Flight can give the [class kits](28-class-resource-loops.md) different openings without making ranged play compulsory. Blade might draw the same creature under an awning, step aside as it dives and cut it while its wings unfold. Let the wingbeat or the shadow telegraph that chance even when a roof hides the body. An enemy that circles beyond reach indefinitely offers no such fight.
