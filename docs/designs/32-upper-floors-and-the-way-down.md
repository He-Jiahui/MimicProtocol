# Upper Floors and the Way Down

Status: Design proposal. Extends [World Structure and Buildings](09-world-structure-and-buildings.md) and [City Terrain](29-presentation-creatures-and-city-terrain.md). [Back to index](README.md)

At the foot of a corporate tower, the name of the archive is still legible on the stair directory. The operator can see the records floor across the atrium. Under the height model in [29](29-presentation-creatures-and-city-terrain.md) that floor is a raised level beside the lobby, not a storey stacked over it: a mezzanine 4 or 8 m up on part of the tower's footprint, reached by an interior stair, because each grid cell can hold only one walkable floor. Ordinary stairs make reaching it a choice: take a landing slowly and listen at the fire door, or cross the exposed flight before a patrol looks up. Higher levels can tend toward better finds because secure work went on there. An executive archive is a reason to climb; an empty cafeteria with a fine view keeps height from becoming a promise of loot.

**The return trip is part of the find.** Carrying an intact recorder, the operator hears something on the stairs. A bridge over a rail cutting or a fenced service yard might reach a different stair core; a skybridge over a walkable street stays scenery, since the street beneath it would need a second walkable layer. A breakable window offers a quicker drop to a stepped plaza one or two levels down, but breaking it can announce the escape; the broken pane becomes a one-way drop cell in the edge's parapet. Show the sill, the landing and the intervening air before commitment, including in the cutaway view, whose cut height must then follow the operator's floor rather than the street. A bridge glimpsed below a window may be too far down to be a sensible landing.

Falls can wound or end a raid: with 4 m levels, one level down hurts, two is a serious wound and three or more can kill. Boots intended for hard landings might make the terrace below a reasonable gamble. A temporary cushioning effect could run out before the return climb. A clip-on descent line makes more sense beside upper-floor evacuation cabinets than in street kiosks: finding one changes which window is interesting, and it needs an anchor above a clear drop to be useful. It replaces the packed parachute first proposed here, because no drop between levels capped at +12 m gives a canopy room to open. The stairs remain a credible exit for someone who finds nothing. Under [Staged Damage Calculation](30-staged-damage-calculation.md), these protections answer a landing impact; collecting similar protections needn't turn every drop into a harmless shortcut.

The height belongs to the architecture. A level street runs along the foot of the terrace; a retaining wall lifts the flat plaza above it. The operator should recognize the same shop entrance looking down from the terrace edge and looking up from the street. A [chosen route](36-routes-to-chosen-activities.md) can point out the stairs without treating an intentional window drop as an ordinary way home.
