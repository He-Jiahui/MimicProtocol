## Why the full bar and the missing payoff can coexist

Charge often looks full in John's play. That doesn't mean the player has the right target, an open firing lane or an infection worth rupturing. In the demo at `81870a5`, the operator starts with one M2 spend. Basic hits, passive recovery, a dodge that absorbs a hit and Brand consumption all replenish the same bank. M2 also has a cooldown. A successful R opens Overdrive, which waives the cost and doubles builder income while M2 still has to be ready. Overflow Valve can even make staying full attractive. There are several reasons to fill the bar while waiting and usually only one button to drain it.

**Source arithmetic, not proposed tuning:** At base, three complete Rifleman bursts landing every round earn `3 × 3 × 4 = 36` Charge; one M2 costs `30`. Five landed Toxic Spits earn `5 × 5 = 25` against that same price. Ignore passive gain, gear, refunds and starting balance to isolate the hits. If all five spits land on one surviving target before Venom expires, the target can be ready for Rupture before those hits have paid for a cloud. Conversely, a Handler can afford a cloud after searching and have nothing infected. Equal prices don't buy equal access to the fun part. These are source examples, not measured time at full across players.

## Four base kits, four things to look for

The following are **proposals**. They would supersede doc 24 sections 3.2–3.3 on two specific points: every M2 need not cost the same Charge, and R need not grant universal free casts. Keep its useful idea that one slot sets up a recognizable result in another. Keep the four inputs and make each base signature accessible before gear or [class levels](39-class-levels-and-barracks-variants.md).

**Rifleman — choose the center of the clear.** Today M1 builds Suppression; Piercing Line spends it and can ricochet. The literal Mark that already causes summon splash belongs to Summoner. Keep the gunner's mark-for-area idea as a shot *the Rifleman* controls: a landed **M1** burst paints a revealed Mimic; aim **M2** through it to release a tight burst around that body. An early mark is enough. At a clinic door, fire as the first pair bunches up, or use **S** to roll across the entrance and line up the rear rank. A few carried cells earned by landing bursts could fund immediate follow-up shots; with none left the ordinary marked shot still returns on its normal recovery. **R** Barrage could spend the saved cells to carry that discharge across successive marked targets while the operator commits to the sweep. Cells buy follow-ups; extra bullets and splash don't earn more cells. Against a lone brute, the marked shot still hits the brute. Frontal plating can reduce that hit heavily, but a sufficiently forceful shot can punch through and clear the escort; a flank saves the investment.

*Barracks choice:* Send the discharge beyond the marked body along the firing line **instead of** around it. A rolled angle is valuable in a queue of enemies; the original splash is easier against a knot beside the doorway. Each recipient resolves its own defense as in [doc 30](30-staged-damage-calculation.md); changing the footprint doesn't also multiply the hit.

**Blade — choose which side of the enemy to finish on.** Today the third cut exposes and Whirl Slash consumes Exposed to shorten cooldowns. Its S refund has no value if Lunge is already ready. Let ordinary aimed **M1** contact build momentum, with the first solid cut knocking a brief guard fragment onto the far side of the hostile, even without a kill. The third cut still opens the guard. **M2** spends momentum on a moving whirl: take the short whirl for space now, or keep cutting to carry it through the pack. Whirling through the open guard spends Exposed to push the hostile aside; holding it leaves a target for Execution. **S** can lunge across the fragment to take a brief shield into the exchange. Lunge can also go straight to safety with nothing to collect. With the guard open, **R** Execution commits Blade to the exposed target; the shield makes the landing worth considering even against a living boss. Momentum cools during a search, and the fragment stays behind. Neither should be needed to start the next fight.

*Barracks choice:* Let Whirl sweep the fragment onto Blade's retreat path, to be caught on the way out with S, **instead of** offering protection on entry. With a wounded operator and the exit at their back that changes the direction of the combo. Repeated cuts on the same opening shouldn't scatter a carpet of shields.

**Summoner — choose which lane still has support when you move.** Today a placed sentry or following drone can consume a grenade Mark on its own. Once hardware is firing, another paid placement needn't be what the room needs. Give the base kit a packed sentry ready for the first fight. **M2** commits it at the player's chosen post; **M1** aims a Marker Grenade to call a shot there. A ready radio order rides on the grenade: the post's next shot at that marked hostile bursts on contact. The radio can recover between orders independently of packed hardware; ordinary grenades still work while it's busy. **S** can hop beside the post to redirect it toward a new lane, or hop away and concede the old one. **R** brings Hive Drones along on the crossing and can carry the next order to a mark out of the post's reach. One order is answered once. Faster sentry fire may answer sooner; it doesn't mint an order per bullet. Packing the post back up recovers the unit immediately and stops its covering fire. If forced to abandon it, recall it from cover: the gun falls silent at once and takes time to return. This leaves a gap in support on the crossing without stranding the base kit until a lucky drop.

*Barracks choice:* Reserve the order for the fixed post even while drones are out, or hand it to the mobile flight. A post can hold fire for a pack about to round a corner; following drones can take the shot after the operator leaves. Both keep the grenade-and-sentry signature when R is unavailable. Neither gets two bursts from one Mark.

**Toxic Handler — choose whether this infection is worth waiting for.** Today Rupture requires a cloud tick on a still-living target at full Venom stacks. Weak quarry can die or walk out before the distinctive hit. Let one aimed **M1** spit leave enough infection for the first contact with **M2** Wither Cloud to cash out a small Rupture. The cloud still threatens the ground when no host is primed. At the stairs, rupture the leader now to open the landing, or use **S** Acid Slide to leave a slowing pool across the pursuit and let further spits build infection. **R** Plague Burst could move part of that unspent infection from the leader into revealed pursuers instead of taking the immediate rupture. Against a lone boss the small rupture still works; neither a spread target nor a kill is its price. Leaving the stairs forfeits the patch, not the right to use the next spit.

*Barracks choice:* Where cloud and pool overlap, keep the host infected and renew its remaining exposure **instead of** rupturing on contact. The stairs may be worth defending; the base release is better when the operator must leave. Following [doc 30](30-staged-damage-calculation.md), only harm still to come is available for conversion. Harm already dealt can't be banked again; transferring it to another host removes that share from the first. More tick speed shouldn't also grow the spendable balance per tick.

## What would distinguish an improvement?

Start with [doc 28](28-class-resource-loops.md)'s small experiment: reduce routine builder income alone, keep the opening spend, and compare the same unmodified kit and encounters. At each intended M2, distinguish **cooldown still running**, **ready but short of Charge**, and **affordable but no useful target**. Record overflow during fights separately from time spent searching. If less Charge merely makes a primed Handler wait longer while a Rifleman still overflows, stop cutting the shared income; try the class opportunities above. A half-empty bar isn't evidence of a better decision.

Then try each base kit in a short street fight without items, at a lone boss without adds, and on a retreat with a bag worth keeping. Look for one ordinary signature before R is needed, a reason sometimes to hold it, and a useful aimed basic and escape when the opportunity is gone. Repeat with the Barracks alternative: ask where the player stood or what they gave up. If the same move wins everywhere but produces a bigger number, revise the branch. Only after the base loop works, review full-Charge, refund and Overdrive gear from doc 24; don't translate every old bonus into extra cells, fragments, orders or infections. [Meta progression](07-meta-progression-and-difficulty.md) can open the alternatives without selling access to a functioning kit.

Repeat the street case beside apparent civilians. Under [doc 37](37-lurkers-among-civilians.md), splash, broad swings, drones, trails and chains must neither harm nor identify a human-looking figure, nor award resources that betray its identity. Basics stay manually aimed in both views; only a deliberate aimed finishing hit on the disguise can cause a damage reveal. The alternative is the rig and its mutual sensing risk. The player who skips the fight and leaves alive with the intel has won. These are proposed comparisons; no gameplay or balance trial was run for this document.
