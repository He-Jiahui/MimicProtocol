# Opening the Lair and Fighting the Brood

Status: Design proposal. Extends [Demo Bosses](22-demo-bosses.md) and [Boss Loot and Drops](23-demo-boss-loot-and-drops.md). [Back to index](README.md)

The Reliquary can be visible from the shelter without its Matron being available on demand. In the existing encounter, `startBoss`, `sporeRain` and `updateBoss` in `src/sim/boss.js` already give her a brood, hatching cysts, a burrow and acid left by the rain. The best reason to expand that fight is what the operator learns by finding her: much of the plaza is already part of the animal's nest.

**Finding a place and opening it are different jobs.** For a known lair, borrow the regional gathering choice of a Helltide-like hunt. A sac from the flooded market and a scent-bearing molt from the rail sheds could make a brood offering the Matron will surface to investigate. Seeing the empty cradle early lets the operator decide whether to cross town, carry the finds, or extract with other salvage. For an unknown site, a detector could sweep a fan and indicate a bearing rather than a destination. Overlapping bearings from detectors at separate street corners could triangulate the source inside a tram depot; roofs and cellars are out of reach on the one walkable street level. A different hunt might start with an interrupted convoy: escort its surviving surveyor far enough to identify the shutter behind which the creature was sealed. These are alternative stories, not chores to require for every boss. A [route indicator](36-routes-to-chosen-activities.md) can lead to a lookout without solving the search.

**The Matron tends a living web.** When a sac pulses, threads tug at nearby creatures and the brood grows restless. Cutting the link might calm the pack; letting the young gather makes a tempting marked shot. A low frontal shell strongly reduces incoming hits. A powerful attack still wounds her through it and can scythe through facing brood guards; moving aside for a clean angle conserves the shot. Her brace suggests a straight rush. Bait her into striking a ground sac and the recoil could lift the shell for a brief stagger. A close sweep catches someone who stays underneath afterward; a rising throat and distending blisters warn of venom spray or a blister about to burst.

**What the player leaves on the floor matters.** An explosive sac can be shot while the lane is clear or dodged and left for the Matron to hit. A burst might leave a persistent venom pool; a flat coil of silk might bind the operator in the path of the next sweep. As spent sacs settle into the rain, lingering burst, poison and binding traps can recede from sight. A deliberate scan briefly picks out their shapes. The player who saw silk laid across the steps can take the dry plinth while the rig recovers. Let the laying motion, the twitch of a strand or the absence of rain ripples leave witnesses to a trap, so scanning buys information instead of paying a toll to survive.

The distinctive threat is what remains *on the operator*. Different venoms could leave an ongoing drain, stiffen the legs or firing arm, or leave the operator more susceptible to a fresh wound or the poison still working. Repeated exposure makes staying in the pool increasingly dangerous. Someone who escapes it can still have good reason to seek cover until the tremor subsides. Following [Staged Damage Calculation](30-staged-damage-calculation.md), give the lingering injury, restriction and susceptibility different jobs. Accumulation can worsen the drain without replaying past harm or letting each symptom multiply every other one.

**Let the nest change the fight.** The Matron might retreat onto overhead webbing, briefly invulnerable while out of reach. That withdrawal should look different from a shot striking her grounded shell. Use the respite to read the floor or break a hanging sac. A tightening strand can signal a diving strike; other sacs hung overhead might drop later to burst or hatch. Early on, the player can learn a single rush or follow a strand to its brood. Later, a sac may fall across the clean way out of a venom spray. Wounds could drive the retreat, a hatching cycle could set its rhythm, or visibly swollen sacs could show the Matron gathering and spending reserves. Choose the rhythm that best explains the creature. A long fight grows harder as recognizable acts overlap, with a way through still visible.

**Other bosses deserve different verbs.** These are possible encounters alongside the existing roster:

- A hooked insect could carve across the plaza in long slashes. The stance of its cutting limb discloses the side it cannot sweep. Its escalation might be losing that limb and learning to pivot on the other; it has no need for the Matron's venom puzzle.
- A deeply infected soldier still moves from cover to cover and spends a visible reserve on practiced combat skills, much as the operator does. A familiar reload is initially a safe opening. Later the human weapon falls silent but the infected arm keeps moving: monstrous mobility changes where the opening ends without erasing the soldier's habits.
- An alien-cult bishop remains planted in a ritual site. Signs on the floor anticipate sweeping beams; human adherents and lurkers draw close when the operator's composure falters under mental influence. Stepping behind a pillar to recover one's bearings or breaking the chant can matter more than chasing a motionless target.
- Link-creatures can project new bodies and recombine. For a shared-life brood, any exposed body offers a way to wound the same organism; a shot across overlapping bodies shouldn't claim the same wound repeatedly. In a different family, separated bodies revive each other unless brought down within a short overlapping window. A visible thread reaching for the fallen partner tells the operator which fight this is.
- A high-dimensional presence might fold an alley toward a pulling singularity. A visible crease could deny a skill on one side and restore it when crossed back; a mirrored surface could return harm until the angle changes. Let the player discover a spatial rule and exploit its safe side before the space shifts again. A scrambled control or an unexplained reflected killing blow teaches nothing about the entity.

Keep victory and departure distinct. The special exfil in [Boss Loot](23-demo-boss-loot-and-drops.md) has a world-changing victory, the B3 kill, as its unlock. Opening an ordinary lair needn't open a free exit, and the Matron's drops still make the walk back a decision under [field extraction](34-raid-pressure-and-field-challenges.md).
