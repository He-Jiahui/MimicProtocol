# Meridian After the Descent

Status: World and copy proposal. Updated: 2026-11-27. Requirements: R-03, R-08, R-09, R-10, R-11.

[Back to index](README.md) | Extends [World and Narrative](01-world-and-narrative.md), [Vision and Pillars](00-vision-and-pillars.md) and [Lurkers Among Civilians](37-lurkers-among-civilians.md). See also [Reading Cities in Games](41-reading-cities-in-games.md).

**Descent Ward** is the proposed English name for the district where the nest-ship came down. It sounds like something a dispatcher could write in the empty address field for a piece of city that no longer fits the old street plan. People inside might still give their old address. Meridian, Mimics, Command and the Lantern remain working names. The Lantern is still in high Earth orbit. The nest-ship that has come down across a stormwater interchange is another vessel. Command can plot both; it cannot say whether the thing on the ground is a passenger, an offspring or an organ.

**Before the ship.** Meridian once raised its bus roads above the floods without buying out the families along the old street below them. The old addresses stayed. A letter to a hillside flat might go in through a tin flap beside a fish counter, even though the family now comes home from the road above. At Dockside, fish sellers marked the last high water on the auction columns. When tenants moved uphill they brought that habit with them. A pencil date on a tall lobby's fine stone is how a newcomer learns whose homes used to stand here. These are possible histories for the districts in 01; the ward adds a place to the city without taking an old name away.

**After the ship.** A shelter dispatcher still files missing people by the door they went out of. One address has a note beside it: *Lower entrance only. If you reach the florist you've gone above her.* The message works as directions long after the terminal stops answering. Near the ship, residents have started adding where the water goes. A rental notice reads: *Dry inside. You can hear it drinking through the party wall.* Something has taken possession of the building's services while the mail still comes through the slot.

**Command on the radio.** Sample briefing: *“Bring back the convalescent hospital's original admissions book. The copy we hold has no discharge column.”* Someone at Command knows which version went missing; the hospital is the soldier's, in [45](45-boss-homes-and-the-descent-ward.md). They still send one operator: the rig's link runs both ways. A bus mechanic at the shelter offers a different brief: *“If you find my brother, ask where he parked. Don't give him the answer.”* That is what a frightened person might try; it cannot authenticate a human. Even the right answer leaves the operator with the choice of trusting him or using the rig and being sensed in return.

**An ordinary search.** The dock office keeps unclaimed keys in a biscuit tin; each tag bears a berth name. A handwritten plea on the lid asks whoever takes the blue key to unstick the canteen shutter on their way home. That is a good reason to open it. The tin might be a Host. Keep the affection and the danger in the same object; giving infected tins a special finish would rob the search of both. A key or the admissions book has a story at the shelter only if the operator gets it there alive.

**What the shelter door promises.** This is the shelter of the later ward map in 45, not the demo's South Sector shelter. The old bus road still leads toward a local pickup. From its first turn you can also walk down to the ship: the barricade faces *along* the lower street, leaving a clear view of what the flood crews abandoned. Command calls it a high-danger posting even for a prepared operator. A person can look from safe ground, decide against the descent and have a worthwhile raid. [The boss homes](45-boss-homes-and-the-descent-ward.md) take up what it would mean to go in.
