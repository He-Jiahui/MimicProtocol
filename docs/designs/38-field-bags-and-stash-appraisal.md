# Field Bags and Stash Appraisal

Status: Design proposal. Extends [Loot and Contamination](08-loot-contamination-and-legendary-gear.md), [Boss Drops](23-demo-boss-loot-and-drops.md) and [Equipment and Loot](26-equipment-loot-reference-brushwild.md). [Back to index](README.md)

Looking for a working receiver ought to send the operator to a different door than looking for medicine. A ferry dispatcher's drawer might hold an intact tide recorder; a sealed tram tool case might contain a power coupler. A clinic cold box can hold an unusually valuable diagnostic sample, but a box still warm in a dead refrigerator deserves a second look. Everyday objects can still be Hosts. A particularly good find should change the question from “what else can I open?” to “which way am I carrying this out?”

**A field bag with distinct places.** A dressing in a quick-use pocket is ready when the operator breaks cover. A recorder in a padded cargo slot is worth getting home. A relay carried in an equipment sleeve is useful now, even if leaving it at the mast means returning with less to sell. Show occupied places and what a find would displace before asking the player to take it. Bag slots are about carrying and access; they are separate from the weapon, body, rig and backpack the operator wears. A run item that already changed the class's play needn't masquerade as loose salvage to sort under fire.

**Let uncertainty be something the player can sell.** Today `sellItem` in `src/sim/profile.js` refuses unidentified gear. Lab processing reveals it; `gearValue` in `src/sim/loot.js` mainly values the item's grade, level, enhancement and special properties rather than the strength of each rolled effect. A Quartermaster could offer cash *as-is*, using only what can be seen on the sealed piece. Two apparently identical sealed rigs shouldn't betray their different contents through the bid. Selling now frees space and saves Lab Samples; opening one might reveal an unremarkable rig or an unusually effective one. Appraisal discovers the existing roll, not a new one.

Once identified, the offer should reflect the strength and usefulness of the revealed effects. A fine rig that holds a mark through a retreat can command a good price even if this operator fights close. A familiar rarity color alone can't explain that price. Use the effect as understood in [Staged Damage Calculation](30-staged-damage-calculation.md): if comparable bonuses gather together in combat, don't price the combination as though each multiplies the whole attack. A sale estimate and a recommendation to equip are different judgments.

**Give the collection some memory.** The hub stash can grow into pages once a single view becomes unwieldy. Returning from the Lab to the page with the field rigs should leave the newly identified piece easy to find; filtering across pages keeps a forgotten item from becoming effectively lost. Use a broader vocabulary of resource icons in the bag, stash and sale offer. A sample ampoule, a wound coil of cable and a notched dispatch key read differently by outline. A seal can denote unknown contents without giving away the effect. Keep names beside unfamiliar silhouettes so a valuable find doesn't depend on reading a hue in the rain.
