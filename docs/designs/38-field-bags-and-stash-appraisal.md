# Field Bags and Stash Appraisal

Status: Design proposal. Extends [Loot and Contamination](08-loot-contamination-and-legendary-gear.md), [Boss Drops](23-demo-boss-loot-and-drops.md) and [Equipment and Loot](26-equipment-loot-reference-brushwild.md). [Back to index](README.md) | Previous: [37 Lurkers Among Civilians](37-lurkers-among-civilians.md) | Next: [39 Class Levels and Barracks Variants](39-class-levels-and-barracks-variants.md)

Looking for a working receiver ought to send the operator to a different door than looking for medicine. A ferry dispatcher's drawer might hold an intact tide recorder; a sealed tram tool case might contain a power coupler. A clinic cold box can hold an unusually valuable diagnostic sample, but a box still warm in a dead refrigerator deserves a second look. Everyday objects can still be Hosts. A particularly good find should change the question from “what else can I open?” to “which way am I carrying this out?”

**A field bag with distinct places.** A dressing in a quick-use pocket is ready when the operator breaks cover. A recorder in the cargo grid is worth getting home. A relay carried in an equipment sleeve is useful now, even if leaving it at the mast means returning with less to sell. Show occupied places and what a find would displace before asking the player to take it. These places are marked regions of the one active pack's grid in [61](61-packing-for-the-walk-out.md); they are about carrying and access, with the same weight and loss as cargo. The pack is the bag itself, not a piece worn beside the weapon, body and rig. (61's grid replaces a list of separate bag slots, which could not say whether a 2-by-2 parcel still fits; “padded” is dropped because [60](60-what-makes-loot-worth-extracting.md) allows no protected pocket.) A run item that already changed the class's play needn't masquerade as loose salvage to sort under fire.

**Let uncertainty be something the player can sell.** Today `sellItem` in `src/sim/profile.js` refuses unidentified gear. Lab processing reveals it; `gearValue` in `src/sim/loot.js` mainly values the item's grade, level, enhancement and special properties rather than the strength of each rolled effect. A Quartermaster could offer cash *as-is*, using only what can be seen on the sealed piece. Two apparently identical sealed parcels shouldn't betray their different contents through the bid. The stored `value` can't serve as that bid: `rollGear` computes it at drop, already counting the Aspect and Prime. Keep the sealed bid below the identified price that grade can expect, so a spare Sample still pays on a good grade and selling sealed wins when Samples are short or the grade is low. Selling now frees space and saves Lab Samples; opening one might reveal an unremarkable rig or an unusually effective one. Appraisal discovers the existing roll, not a new one.

Once identified, the offer should reflect the strength and usefulness of the revealed effects. A fine rig that holds a mark through a retreat can command a good price even if this operator fights close. A familiar rarity color alone can't explain that price. Use the effect as understood in [Staged Damage Calculation](30-staged-damage-calculation.md): if comparable bonuses gather together in combat, don't price the combination as though each multiplies the whole attack. A sale estimate and a recommendation to equip are different judgments. Neither an enhancement nor an affix reroll should raise the offer by more than it cost, or the Armory becomes a mint ([26](26-equipment-loot-reference-brushwild.md) shows today's enhancement case).

**Give the collection some memory.** The hub stash can grow into pages once a single view becomes unwieldy. Returning from the Lab to the page with the field rigs should leave the newly identified piece easy to find; filtering across pages keeps a forgotten item from becoming effectively lost. Use a broader vocabulary of resource icons in the bag, stash and sale offer. A sample ampoule, a wound coil of cable and a notched dispatch key read differently by outline. A seal can denote unknown contents without giving away the effect. Keep names beside unfamiliar silhouettes so a valuable find doesn't depend on reading a hue in the rain.
