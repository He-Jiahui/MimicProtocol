# Meridian Map Families and Crossings

Status: Design proposal. Extends [World Structure](09-world-structure-and-buildings.md), [Procedural Generation](10-procedural-generation.md) and [Presentation and City Terrain](29-presentation-creatures-and-city-terrain.md). [Back to index](README.md)

A familiar city needn't mean a memorized shopping route. The fixed introductory district in [Raid Map, Time of Day and Zones](17-demo-raid-map-time-and-zones.md) can teach the journey from shelter to pickup. Beyond that introduction, vary the safe insertion area, street connections, interiors and regional danger from raid to raid. A depot shelter might be safe on arrival while the container yard beyond it is overrun; on a return raid the shelter might be beside the market and the dangerous route runs under the viaduct. Let the operator read those differences before committing. A new seed shouldn't invalidate the survey work on a named sector in [Contracts and Sector States](21-demo-contracts-and-sector-states.md).

**Author the character of a setting; vary the routes it supports.** Keep Meridian's rain, neon and level places to stand. Stairs, retaining walls, piers, overpasses and building platforms carry changes in elevation. Different settings should offer more than different facades:

- **Urban core.** Tall towers share service lobbies, loading courts and [upper-floor routes](32-upper-floors-and-the-way-down.md). A lit skybridge may link an archive tower to a hotel; on another visit a shutter at its end sends the operator down past the kitchens. The plaza below stays flat enough to read a charge across it. A rooftop objective makes the position of the public stairs worth remembering.
- **Residential streets.** Lower housing blocks surround a plaza and snack street. An underpass can lead into municipal sewers; a villa street on a higher terrace offers a quieter way around. Dense shrubs spill through garden railings, trees crowd an abandoned courtyard, and trampled openings show where people still walk. A civilian escort can follow a well-lit arcade or use the dry service passage below. A familiar district can feel changed by which of those routes is occupied.
- **Harbor.** The river opens to the sea past a lighthouse. Moored ships interrupt sightlines along the quay, packed containers form aisles, and intricate industrial plant links pump halls, pipe galleries and service gantries. Beyond the fence, runway and airfield apron offer an exposed alternative to the freight maze. The lighthouse keeps its value as a bearing even when the usable gangway, rail gate and sheltered route to the launch change.

**Crossing is a wager; extraction ends it.** An elite might guard a freight crossing into the harbor. Defeating or getting past the guard gives access to the next in-raid map with the same bag at risk. Crossing advances raid difficulty: Command might warn that the freight alarm has brought the later patrols forward. The pressure persists across the boundary rather than resetting to a fresh opening or stacking an unrelated combat multiplier. The player can decline the crossing and work toward a pickup nearby.

A marked extraction point still needs local work, as in [Field Challenges](34-raid-pressure-and-field-challenges.md): reach a radio to bring the launch alongside, or clear the ramp for the [evacuation van](31-presentation-craft-and-interactables.md). A [boss offering or detector fix](35-opening-the-lair-and-fighting-the-brood.md) opens an encounter, not a general travel permit. Leave a discoverable, reachable way to earn departure without crossing an elite gate or awakening a boss. A shelter is safe ground on insertion, not an automatic cash-out. On a good seed the hardest question at the pier is whether the operator wants to leave, not whether the map happened to generate the missing key behind the exit it unlocks.
