# A Street in the Rain

Status: Scene and material proposal. Updated: 2026-11-27. Requirements: R-02, R-03, R-07, R-09, R-13.

[Back to index](README.md) | Extends [Rendering and Visual Style](11-rendering-and-visual-style.md), [Presentation, Creatures and City Terrain](29-presentation-creatures-and-city-terrain.md) and [Presentation Craft and Interactables](31-presentation-craft-and-interactables.md). World context: [Meridian After the Descent](42-meridian-after-the-descent.md).

Consider the lower entrance of an apartment tower on wash day. An operator looking down the street should be able to guess which window belongs to the laundry, where the tenants enter and how a repair van could get a broken machine out. Look at the same facade with its lights switched off. If all the windows and doors could trade places without anyone inside noticing, another layer of detail on the walls won't make a building of it.

**Give the front a back.** The laundry floor sits level with the unloading court. Through a broad side door you can see the folding bench and the turn a trolley makes around it. The tenants' entrance is up a short stair, out of the runoff; letterboxes are recessed where someone can stand to read them. Above, a deeper beam spans the shop opening and the flats rise on narrower bays. Some balconies are glazed in, but the slab edges and rain outlets still line up. From the shoulder these junctions give the walls weight; from above the offset between stair and loading threshold tells you which door reaches the street with a full bag. These are relationships the [procedural buildings](10-procedural-generation.md) could share while looking quite different.

**Let physically based materials answer the same light.** Imagine one amber laundry sign reflected down the court. Its letters hold together in standing water, scatter across damp broom-finished concrete and disappear on the sheltered grit under the bench. The van door carries a highlight in its clear coat; a gouge through the paint exposes a different metal edge. Behind the windshield the pale driver's seat remains visible where the awning cuts the glare. Water runs from a sill onto the brick below it; mortar stays dark after the raised brick faces begin to dry. The ambition is a convincing PBR appearance across the whole street. A sheet of gloss on everything would erase information about shelter, depth and footing.

**Ease the bloom slightly.** For this presentation direction, that supersedes the “strong bloom” wording in [R-02](00-vision-and-pillars.md) and doc 11's visual target, and qualifies its “wet everything” shorthand. Keep the Ghostwire lighting and atmosphere reference, Meridian's teal and amber signs, bright tubes, wet-road reflections and depth in the rain. At shoulder height the laundry sign has a soft fringe; you can still read the chipped enamel between the letters. From tactical height the reflected amber stops at the curb and a warning on the paving beyond stays distinct. Night can feel brighter as shops switch on without making a revealed creature vanish against them.

| At the laundry court | What makes it belong there |
| --- | --- |
| A delivery van | The sill meets the porter's trolley at a plausible height. A loaded rear sits lower on its springs; tread meets wet stone beneath the wheel arch. Only the sliding-door runner is polished from use. Parked nose out with unloading room at the rear, it leaves the tenants' stair mouth clear. |
| A street tree | Its trunk flares into a lifted edge of the tree pit; roots have buckled an old paver that someone reset. The crown leans out from the balcony shade. Wet outer leaves pick up the sign while inner branches stay dark. From above the gap pruned for the doorway leaves the approach visible. |
| The operator and a porter | The operator's pack pulls on the shoulder straps and clears the hips when crouching. The porter leans back against a laden cart, boots planted downhill of its wheels. Soaked cloth hangs off the elbows; the dry fold under a strap stays light. Those differences in weight carry farther than facial detail. |
| People waiting out the rain | Someone folds an umbrella before squeezing past the cart; another steps off the curb to make room and gets a wet trouser cuff. Give a disguised Mimic the same convincing gait, contact and clothing wear. An awkward pause in the queue is cause to look, never proof. |
| A bench and a lost-property basket | Tenants sit at the end nearest the dryer vent; the paint there is worn down to timber. The wicker basket fits underneath without blocking their feet. It could hold a useful find or be a Host. Neither possibility calls for its own pool of decorative light. |

From above, keep the space between van and stair large enough to read a person's direction of travel and aim deliberately. At shoulder height let the operator see the far curb past the van mirror. An ordinary person in that sightline remains an ordinary-looking person: area effects cannot test the queue. A scan carries the risk of mutual detection; a damage reveal takes the deliberate finishing hit described in [Lurkers Among Civilians](37-lurkers-among-civilians.md). Basic attacks still depend on the operator's aim.
