## 1. Premise

On an ordinary day an unidentified vessel appears in high Earth orbit with no launch, no signal and no warning. Within hours, strange organisms, the Mimics, are present in the largest cities. They do not rampage. They disguise themselves as ordinary citizens, copying appearance, gait and daily routine well enough that no ordinary person, camera or database can tell the difference.

Working names (all placeholders): the vessel is the Lantern; the organisms are Mimics; the player's military organization is Command; the city is Meridian.

## 2. The sense rig

- Command recovered a prototype device from early containment work. Worn on the back of the hand and along the spine, it detects the bioelectric signature that Mimics cannot hide.
- Resonance rule (R-09): the rig and a Mimic's signature couple in both directions. Anything the rig resolves also resolves the rig in return. An idle Mimic walking its patrol route has no interest in anyone until the link forms; then it becomes aware of the operator and turns hostile.
- This rule is the reason for the solo mission. A squad multiplies the signature and wakes the whole district. One operator running low-power is the only plausible insertion (R-10).
- Gameplay consequences are specified in [03](03-mutants-detection-and-enemy-ai.md).

## 3. Mission framing (R-10)

You are the only operative Command can send. Each raid is a contract package:

- Primary: recover specific intel (data shards, dossiers, lab samples) for Command.
- Secondary: scavenge supplies and equipment for yourself and for trade.
- Constraint: reach an active extraction point alive. Carried loot is only secured on extraction.

Command is a distant voice on a radio link: bureaucratic, competent and not fully honest. Its briefings are the main vehicle for lore, with environmental details and recovered dossiers as supporting channels.

## 4. Infection of the everyday (R-11)

Mimic biomass spreads through objects, not just bodies. Vending machines, rice cookers, lockers, street food stalls, server racks and medical cabinets may be Hosts: apparently ordinary containers that have been colonized. Opening a Host can reveal good loot, a trap, an ambush, or an infection that corrupts what you carry. Rules are in [08](08-loot-contamination-and-legendary-gear.md).

## 5. Setting: Meridian

Meridian is a fictional, dense, vertical, always-raining megacity. It is not a real city and must not copy a real district layout.

| District | Character | Gameplay role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neon Strip | Arcades, narrow eatery alleys, signage canyons | High loot density, heavy crowds, loud |
| Arcology Heights | Residential megatowers, sky bridges | Vertical interiors, long sightlines, safe rooms |
| Dockside Logistics | Container yards, cranes, warehouses | Open fights, vehicle cover, extraction by water |
| Old Town Alleys | Low-rise warrens, shrines, laundry lines | Tight stealth, ambush risk |
| Transit Hub | Stations, tunnels, trains | Underground routes, extraction by rail |
| Corporate Plaza | Glass towers, lobbies, server floors | Intel-heavy, elite-heavy, reflective visuals |
| Foundry Belt | Heavy industry, steam, molten glow | Hazard-heavy, strong emissive lighting |

A raid uses one district or a seam between two (see [09](09-world-structure-and-buildings.md)).

## 6. Tone and style rules

- Cyber-noir dread: quiet streets, buzzing signs, rain, distant sirens, then sudden violence.
- The player is competent but outnumbered; victory is leaving, not clearing.
- Civilians exist in the city as evacuees in shelters and as stragglers (Proposal). A Mimic wearing a civilian face is the central uncertainty.
- Harming real civilians is penalized by Command (see [03](03-mutants-detection-and-enemy-ai.md)).
