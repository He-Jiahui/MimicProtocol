# Boss Homes and the Descent Ward

**Matron of Hollows: the Reliquary works.** Leave the little shrine where the workers put it, by the gate. Beyond it stands a cable works whose covered winding hall once needed an uninterrupted span between drum stands. The spider has taken that entire volume. Silk fans from crane rails to the high windows; a loaded strand draws taut and shakes dust from a distant rail before the Matron swings across open air. From the shoulder you can follow it to the suspended body. From above, you can see her shadow travel over the winding floor and a hanging sac pull sideways over the bay where it might land. The fight itself stays on one floor level, as the height model in [29](29-presentation-creatures-and-city-terrain.md) requires: a swing is a move between two points on the floor drawn as an arc under its strand, she is out of reach while she hangs, and her landing and every falling sac are marked on the floor before they arrive. Leave a bare strip where workers rolled finished drums out: the operator needs to see the next rush even while watching the roof.

For a later authored Reliquary, the enclosed works supersedes doc 22's round open shrine plaza as B1's fighting space; `placeLandmarks` in `src/sim/world.js` builds that plaza today, a reserved circle with eight pillars and six lantern poles. The shrine remains a place to bring a brood offering if that lair story is chosen. Finding it needn't wake her. Keep doc 35's broods, sacs, venom and the difference between being out of reach in the web and taking reduced damage through her grounded shell. A heavy shot can still punch through the front of a brood group. From the gate the operator can also walk on toward extraction with the offering still in the bag.

**The infected soldier: a convalescent hospital.** A street entrance serves families, an ambulance ramp meets the treatment floor, and a glazed recovery gallery overlooks a therapy court. A row of hooks once let patients hang their coats before practicing the walk back to a bus stop painted on the far wall. A service door behind the painting leads to a military observation room. There, the soldier's request to leave is clipped to a trial sheet. The observer recorded whether the patient could still find the painted stop after exposure to a recovered Mimic fragment. A civilian hospital lent Command a ward for recovery; someone kept the trial going after the patient withdrew consent. Let the soldier's own record be worth carrying out even if the operator leaves the encounter alone.

The soldier still puts their back to a doorjamb to reload. From the far end of the gallery the weapon falls quiet and the free arm keeps its grip on the lintel as the rest of the body swings through. The building lets practiced human movement and its infected extension occupy the same sightline. The gallery runs a level above the court along one side rather than over it, so the swing is a telegraphed, boss-only crossing of that edge that the operator can follow only by the stair. Visitors' benches line the sides of the therapy court; the walking surface down its middle stays clear. This develops the additional soldier idea in 35. B2 **The Reflection** keeps its Corporate Plaza server atrium and its contest of disguises, scans and echoes from 22. A patient or passerby in the hospital is no more fair game for a probing blast than a pedestrian outside.

**The Herald: walk out into the Descent Ward.** The shelter door sticks on a clean rubber sweep. Outside you hear the same steady rain as inside, then a second patter from *below* the road. The bus road bends toward a familiar pickup; a short stair on the other side descends toward the damaged interchange. At its head a civil warning board still carries the last crew's chalk: *Water coming back under pressure. Do not enter the lower concourse.* Past it, lamps disappear in succession beneath a contracting deck. On the grid the ward is sunken: the lower concourse sits one or two levels below the street under a deck that is scenery, and the survey walk runs a level above the chamber floor along its edge. Command warns of extreme danger while the shelter door is still in view. This is an optional early approach to a difficult region. The shelter stays safe, and taking the bus road leaves an ordinary raid ahead.

From the stair, the grounded nest-ship looks like the retaining wall until a row of bolted pump housings lifts with its breathing. A dry line along the brick shows where the old pavement sat. Deeper in, pipe flanges grip tendon, the hillside's inspection doors open onto the inside of the hull, and the roof of the flood chamber draws tight when the inlet below it drinks. The unnerving scale comes from recognizing an entire piece of public infrastructure as a working part of something alive. Keep one human reference in each view: a stair rail continuous to the landing, or the enamel tide gauge that can be seen again from the upper walk. Strange space is more unsettling when the operator knows what the room used to do.

At the chamber's far end a gap in the hull frames the Lantern, still overhead. When its light moves, the nearest seams in the deck close; farther away a figure seems to stand up along the same line. Is the Herald a body, or is this where the link has learned to stand? A visible crease crossing the floor gives the operator room to step aside; the survey walk above gives a reason to look back at the formation before choosing to approach it. For a later map the ward's ship and chamber supersede the Arcology crown as B3's *home*. The crown in doc 22 can remain the demo setting and a city vantage on the beam, built as a podium roof no higher than 12 m or a plaza at the tower's foot, since the height model in 29 does not reach a tower top. Keep the Herald's alien link and the idea of a special exfil for a world-changing victory in [Boss Loot](23-demo-boss-loot-and-drops.md). An ordinary search in the ward can still end at an earned pickup without calling the Herald.

**How much city the demo needs to promise.** The starting point is the South Sector introduction in 17: a fixed introductory geography (one 500 m district in the build, `BLOCKS = 5` in `src/sim/data.js`, although 17 still says 3.2 km), a shelter, a dusk-to-night raid and regions of increasing danger. Its live style regions are Old Town Alleys, Neon Strip, Arcology Heights, Corporate Plaza and Dockside. Transit Hub and Foundry Belt belong to the broader world in 01; they are not additional live styles. Descent Ward is a proposed new scene, not a new name for a danger band. Its short approach from shelter is a later authored exception to the demo's gradual danger geography, not a change to the introductory route. It would be its own authored map holding its shelter, bus road and sunken interchange together, entered at insertion or through a [crossing](40-meridian-map-families-and-crossings.md) from another map, because the demo district is generated and keeps only one 100 m block of safe ground around its Shelter, too little to hold a sunken interchange as well.

The useful near-term question is whether a player at a shelter can describe the dry way home and point to the place they are reluctant to enter. A street corner with a visible return stair could answer that before a whole tower interior exists. Leave the joined towers, the working waterfront and the descent through the hull for maps where both ends of those journeys can be authored. A web painted on a roof cannot promise an aerial nest, any more than a view of the Lantern can promise an encounter with the Herald. Settle what brought people to each place and what they left behind before settling a boss sequence or filling the skyline.
